If you have not gone yet, you can still go.

The Wall That Heals is at the Belmont County Fairgrounds, 45420 Roscoe Road in
St. Clairsville, and the grounds are free and open 24 hours today, Saturday,
October 3. Taps are played at dusk. The closing ceremony is at 1:45 p.m. Sunday,
October 4, and the exhibit closes at 2 p.m., when the ceremony ends.

The host committee's published schedule and the Belmont County Tourism
Council's event listing agree on the address, on 24-hour public access and on the
2 p.m. Sunday close. The committee's schedule is what sets the ceremony times
above. There is no fee to enter the grounds.

## What is on the fairgrounds

The Wall That Heals is a traveling, three-quarter-scale replica of the Vietnam
Veterans Memorial in Washington, D.C. It carries the 58,281 names of the service
members who did not come home from Vietnam. The exhibit is built to be walked,
one name at a time, and the host committee notes that visitors, veterans,
families, schools and the public may move through the outdoor memorial at their
own pace.

The national partner on the Belmont County stop is the Vietnam Veterans
Memorial Fund. The presenting sponsor is American Legion St. Clairsville Post
159. The committee lists Belmont County, Belmont County Fair, Belmont County
Tourism and Rotary of St. Clairsville as Freedom Sponsors, and UPMC and Whiteside
Chevrolet among its Tribute Sponsors.

Jackee Pugh is the executive director of the Belmont County Tourism Council, the
organization listed as the organizer of this stop.

## Two days left

Because the exhibit stays open around the clock, there is no gate to arrive
before and no closing hour to work around tonight. You can come early evening,
late evening, or in the middle of the afternoon, and the wall is there.

Saturday's one scheduled moment is Taps at dusk. Sunday is the short end. The
closing ceremony begins at 1:45 p.m., and the fairgrounds close at 2 p.m., so a
family that wants to see the ceremony should be on the grounds before 1:45.

The host committee schedules group tours of the site, and both the committee
and the tourism council describe the grounds as accessible throughout each public
day. That is the practical point of a 24-hour exhibit. A shift worker finishing
at midnight, a student with a late game, a family with children in bed and a
Sunday morning that will not stretch to 1:45 in the afternoon all have a version
of this visit that works.

If Saturday has been the week that got away from you, tonight is open and there
is nothing to buy and nothing to book.

## One caution on what you may have read

You may have seen turnout figures from this week's opening events. Belmont News
has not confirmed a crowd count, and we are not going to pass one along until a
named person puts it on the record. The address and the round-the-clock access
come from both organizations running the stop, and the ceremony times come from
the host committee's published schedule. Those are the parts you need in order to
plan your visit.

The traveling memorial moves on after Sunday. After the grounds close at 2 p.m.,
the next Belmont County visit is the next one.
